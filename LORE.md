# Goblins of the Ashborn Isles

In the early years of the fourteenth century, fire rose from the Atlantic. Months of eruptions left behind black volcanic islands. When the smoke cleared, goblins already walked their shores. No one saw them arrive. Some captains claim the mountains birthed them; others speak of passages beneath the earth that have since collapsed. Fishing camps became villages, crude mines opened in the ridges, and rival crews fought over sheltered harbors. By 1337, Hooktooth has become Cindermaw's capital, but the Ashborn Isles remain divided. The Brineward of Brackmaw and the Reefhook, Shatterfin and Sootwake clans share the Emberblood's faith in the Hunger Below, yet follow their own crowns; Shatterfin alone keeps the maternal house of the Tidemothers. Poor treasuries, crowded settlements and growing fleets drive them toward expansion. The Ironfang ruler dreams first of uniting the islands. Beyond them lies a world the goblins have only begun to discover.

## Brackmaw — Brineward

Murgash Brackmaw, the Sluice-King, earned his name during the Blackwater Flood. He ordered his own hall's embankment cut to spare Brackhaven's granaries, then bound the feuding marsh houses to rebuild the tidal gates together. Patient, exacting and fiercely proud of his house, he remembers every debt and inspects the sluices himself. His enemies say he prices friendship by the barrel. Murgash answers that hungry allies cannot hold a causeway.

Brackmaw's strength runs through its waterways. Raised roads join the reedlands to Brackhaven, where timber, rope and preserved fish provision the coast. The marsh houses guard their own gates and demand a voice whenever the crown calls for labor. Murgash seeks to make Brackhaven the indispensable harbor of the Ashborn Isles: every pact supplied from its storehouses, every fleet dependent on its workshops. Cindermaw's appetite for timber and provisions offers both a market and a point of pressure. Yet a neglected embankment or a house denied its due can close the channels on which his authority rests.

## Reefhook — Reefstrider

Skrezz Reefhook, the Wreck-Taker, won his name at the Lantern Shoals. With the harbor boats scattered by a squall, he led a relief crew through a submerged channel and brought home the stranded fishers, then stripped their abandoned hulls before the next tide. He made the salvaged timber into boats for households that had lost their catch. Quick with a joke and quicker to spot a bargaining weakness, Skrezz cultivates visiting captains personally. He spends tomorrow's pearl revenue freely and leaves his court to settle the accounts.

House Reefhook traces its authority to Zrekk's first chain of rescue beacons, maintained by each harbor household in return for pilotage rights. Its saying is 'Bring the crew home; reckon the cargo after.' Skrezz wears the house's split-shell clasp rather than a jeweled crown. His older brother Krizzek is the adult dynastic successor under the existing strength law; in court he presses the claims of fishing households against Skrezz's costly foreign friendships. Consort Zikka of House Shoalcut brings ties to the pilot families. Their young children Zikrat and Skrizza make the future of the rescue line a matter of public concern.

Reefhook's growing settlements need safe fishing grounds, dependable buyers and grain from larger islands. Pearl wealth arrives in bursts, while nets and boats need repair every season. Skrezz wants the other crowns to treat Reefhook's pilots as indispensable partners. Harbor elders fear that selling their soundings will surrender the very advantage that keeps the island independent. Divers demand a fair share of rich finds; widowed households expect the house to honor its rescue obligations even in a lean year. Reefhook can turn navigation and trade into influence, but every lost boat tests the crown's claim to rule.

## Sootwake — Ashveil

Snikh Sootwake, the Blackbough, earned his name in the Red Hollow fire. When smoke cut off the ridge settlements, he held a narrow firebreak while families escaped along the charcoal tracks. He carried a scorched branch into his father's hall and later kept it above his seat as a reminder of the crown's debt to those families. Spare with words and suspicious of visiting envoys, he walks boundary lines with the wardens and knows which paths can carry an armed company. He is patient with saplings and harsh with anyone who breaks a cutting oath.

House Sootwake descends from Sizh's band of hearth keepers, who joined scattered clearings through guarded paths and common firebreaks. Its saying is 'Roots outlast fire.' The family seats its children at an unpainted rootwood table, where each generation cuts a new mark beside the old ones. Snikh's older brother Zhor is the adult dynastic successor under the existing strength law and argues that the house owes its wardens generous timber rights. Consort Zheska of House Cinderhush speaks for the charcoal settlements that need the same trees. Their children Khash and Khazra inherit a house whose unity depends on balancing those claims.

Sootwake's expanding settlements give the crown more hands, hearths and mouths to feed. Timber and charcoal can buy independence, yet selling too much leaves bare hills and exposed roads. Snikh wants enough cleared land to feed his people while preserving a forest through which invaders must follow paths his wardens know. Brackmaw's workshops and Cindermaw's forges both want Ashveil wood; their contracts can enrich the kingdom or divide its court. Woodland households defend their cutting rights, and the crown cannot demand endless labor without losing the trust that keeps its scattered settlements together.
